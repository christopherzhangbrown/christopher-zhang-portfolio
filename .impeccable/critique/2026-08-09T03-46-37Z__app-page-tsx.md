## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** This is a beautifully executed instance of the most-produced developer-portfolio template of the last three years. Near-black ground, one warm accent, geometric display face set enormous with tight negative tracking, mono-uppercase eyebrows, hairline dividers instead of cards, fade-up arrivals, custom cursor with lagging ring, ambient particles, scroll-progress bar. Every decision is defensible. Not one is *his*.

The test is literal: replace "Christopher Zhang." with any other two-word name and nothing else on the page needs to change. The five section titles are the five default slots of the genre, in default order, with a period appended. The label rail says ROLE / LOCATION / STATUS — the schema, not the person.

Genuinely specific, and it is a short list: the numbered-report IA (`001 — Education` … `/01` row indices) is a real committed conceit; the amber telemetry curve under the name is the one piece of custom illustration and the best thing on the page; the terminal footer is a genuine idea whose execution is unreliable.

The site's actual differentiator per PRODUCT.md — "ships real AI systems, not demos," backed by Bedrock/Terraform/Argo CD/Go-client-go deployment surface at the undergraduate level — has no expression in the visual system at all. That sentence, or any equivalent, appears nowhere on the site.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` returned exit 2 with 44 findings, all advisory/quality, from only two rules: `design-system-color` ×40 and `design-system-font-size` ×4. All 40 color hits are in `components/stack.tsx` (lines 58–92) and are the official brand colors of the technologies being listed plus their 12% tints — a documented, intentional exception in DESIGN.md. Set those aside and the CLI signal is **4 real findings**: 10px font sizes at `stack.tsx:138` and `app/projects/[id]/page.tsx:241`, and 0.8rem at `components/ui/calendar.tsx:90,99` (unused shadcn primitive).

In-page detector: 16 anti-patterns. After discarding three false-positive families (`ai-color-palette` "cyan neon" — the tokens resolve to near-achromatic blue-grey at chroma 0.005–0.01, there is no cyan; `overused-font` — flags Space Grotesk at 17% and Inter at 20% when mono carries ~63%, so nothing dominates; `clipped-overflow-container` — the container is the decorative `pointer-events-none fixed inset-0` backdrop where clipping is intended), the surviving signal is: `tiny-text` (10px), `pulsing-dot` ×2, `layout-transition` (nav animates `padding` at 300ms, confirmed running), `kicker-above-heading` ×4 and `hero-eyebrow-chip` ×1.

The eyebrow findings are where detector and design review agree from opposite directions: the detector calls the numbered kickers a saturated pattern; the review calls them one of only three genuinely specific things on the page. **The brief wins — they stay.** They are load-bearing identity, documented as The Numbered Section Rule.

## Overall Impression

The craft is real and the concept is coherent. The problem is not quality, it is **allocation**. The page spends its largest typographic gesture — 112px display type — on "Academic background." and its smallest — 16px inside a bullet list — on "$800K annually" and "7 steps to 1 across 4 standardized service templates," which are the only two facts on the site no other applicant can claim.

Measured against the stated metric, recall, the honest verdict is that there isn't a hook. At 40 seconds a recruiter sees a big name, "AI Engineer Intern," "Brown University," and a colorful grid of ~20 logos including HTML and CSS. Tomorrow they remember "Brown, AI, dark site" — recall of a category, not of a person.

The single biggest opportunity is the first viewport. It currently contains zero differentiating information.

## What's Working

**1. The three-voice type system is real and disciplined.** Space Grotesk at 128px with -0.05em tracking against JetBrains Mono at 11.2px with 0.18em is roughly a 12:1 size ratio with opposing tracking directions. That contrast — not the color, not the motion — is why the page reads as authored rather than assembled. The voices never trade jobs across all five sections.

**2. Hairline-and-grid structure with zero cards.** Because nothing is boxed, the Experience bullets — the densest and most valuable text on the site — are the only elements with real weight in their region, so they read as substance rather than filler inside a card.

**3. The status vocabulary is coherent and correctly scarce, and the focus story is genuinely good.** Verified: 22 focusable elements, correct tab order, a visible amber focus ring on every one at 11.31:1 against the ground with 3px offset. All 5 project rows are real buttons, keyboard-reachable and activatable. The One Signal Rule is honored in the implementation, not just the document.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The positioning claim is absent from the first viewport, and from the site
**What:** The hero paragraph re-states the name that is already 128px above it, leads with school and degree, and buries the only load-bearing fact — AI Engineer at Scout Motors — in the final clause. No number appears above the fold. "Ships real AI systems" appears nowhere in any form.
**Why it matters:** This is the memorability metric failing at the only place it can be won. Category information cannot produce recall.
**Fix:** Replace the paragraph with claim-then-proof, both numbers verbatim, name not repeated. Change `Status: Open to opportunities` to name the cycle (`Summer 2027 · SWE internships`). Add `RESUME ↗` to the nav.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Mobile is a materially weaker product, not the same product with less chrome
**What:** `nav.tsx:44` removes all five section links below 768px with no replacement. `projects.tsx:124` deletes every project blurb on mobile, so Projects becomes five rows of title + tags + arrow. `hero.tsx:25` puts ~250px of ROLE/LOCATION/STATUS metadata above the name. `experience.tsx:95` right-aligns the job title below four dense bullets. The nav "Available" pill measures 124×34 at a true 390px viewport, below the 44px guideline.
**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md states forwarded links open on phones. On a phone, the section carrying the "ships real systems" proof has had its proof removed.
**Fix:** Show the blurb at all widths, stacked under the title below `md`. Add a minimal mobile nav. Reorder the hero rail below the name on mobile. Raise the pill to 44px.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Accessibility: the structural system is below contrast threshold and reduced-motion is a false promise
**What:** Three findings sharing one root — decoration overriding user affordances. All independently confirmed by both assessments.
- **Hairline is 1.38:1** (`oklch(0.28 0.008 250)` on `oklch(0.13 0.005 250)`, canvas-resolved). WCAG 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for UI component boundaries. Every divider, border, icon square and button outline is below threshold. Text is fine — muted 5.56:1, signal 11.31:1, body 18.45:1. **The problem is the structure, not the type.**
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` does not suppress the primary motion vocabulary.** There is no `MotionConfig` anywhere in the repo, so framer-motion defaults to `reducedMotion: "never"`. The global CSS override caps only CSS animations (24 of them) and transitions (13). Every `whileInView` fade-up, the hero's 80px word rises, the 2.5–3s SVG path draws and the scroll parallax still play at full duration. DESIGN.md's claim that the override collapses all animation is false for exactly the motion a sensitive user would notice most.
- **Three unlabeled links** — the hero's GitHub / LinkedIn / Mail icon squares have no text and no `aria-label`. There is not one `aria-label` in any of the fifteen reviewed components.
- Related: `* { cursor: none !important }` computes on 552 of 552 elements. It is correctly reverted under reduced-motion and coarse pointers, but a low-vision mouse user who has not set reduced-motion loses their OS cursor-size and high-contrast-cursor settings to a 16px decorative dot.
**Fix:** Lift hairline to ≈`oklch(0.40 0.008 250)`; add `<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">` in `layout.tsx`; add `aria-label` to the three icon links; gate `cursor: none` behind a contrast/pointer-precision query as well.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P2] The designed ending is unreliable, and it is in the wrong place regardless
**What:** The terminal footer typing `christopher@zhang:~$ still building` is the only moment on the page with a voice, and it is the last impression — the highest-leverage position under the peak-end rule. Under a throttled background tab it demonstrably did not complete (`footer.innerText === "_\n© 2026 Christopher Zhang."` after 10s). Two structural causes are real independent of the tab state: `useInView(ref, {margin: "-40px"})` shrinks the observation root by 40px on every side, putting the trigger marginally out of range at the page's own bottom; and the typing is a chained per-character `setTimeout` re-created on every render, which throttles to ~1/s in a background tab. **Not yet confirmed in a foreground tab** — needs a 15-second check.
**Why it matters:** Even when it fires, it sits at the bottom of a 7.2-viewport page that a 90-second recruiter structurally never reaches.
**Fix:** Drop the negative margin (or use `amount: "some"`) and drive the typing from a single `setInterval`. Then reconsider whether this beat belongs only at the bottom.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable animate`

### [P2] The site is starving itself of its own best copy, and hiding its strongest proof
**What:** `projects.tsx` defines a `detail` field for all five projects — five specific engineering sentences ("temporal filtering, plausibility checks, adaptive confidence thresholds, and limb-label correction") — that is **rendered nowhere**, while `/03`, `/04` and `/05` display 5–8-word blurbs. Separately, the AI Job Hunting Agent has a live demo (in the case study data) that is entirely unsignalled on the home page — the single strongest possible proof of "ships real systems, not demos."
**Why it matters:** The evidence that supports the positioning already exists in the codebase and is not on screen.
**Fix:** Render `detail` in the project row or on hover; add a live-demo marker to the project list.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Minor Observations

- `stack.tsx:125` — `whileHover={{ x: 4, filter: "brightness(1.15)" }}` violates DESIGN.md's "Don't animate position or scale on hover for content elements."
- `CursorTrail` calls `setCursor` on every mousemove, runs `document.elementFromPoint` (forced layout) on every move, **and** runs a rAF loop calling `setTrail` every frame — a full React re-render per frame, permanently, over a rotating 1600px conic gradient. The most expensive thing on the site, and pure decoration.
- Three of the eight ambient nodes sit at `top: 130% / 180% / 230%` inside a `fixed inset-0` container — permanently outside the viewport, never rendered.
- The ambient SVG uses `viewBox="0 0 1600 4000"` with `preserveAspectRatio="none"` in a fixed viewport box, squashing geometry ~5:1. The "dashed connector lines" render as near-horizontal streaks, not the schematic DESIGN.md describes.
- Education and Experience each track hover in React state *and* with an identical CSS `:hover` rule — a full re-render per tag hover for no visual gain.
- "HTML" and "CSS" beside Kubernetes, Bedrock and Terraform actively lowers the perceived level for the primary audience.
- `twitter:card` is `summary_large_image` but no OG image is set, so forwarded links — how the referral audience first meets the page — render a blank card. The description says "portfolio of projects, experience, and contact links," which says nothing.
- Three near-empty viewports of ambient dots between sections are dead time on a 90-second read.
- Section index labels are `items-end`-aligned, so `001 — EDUCATION` floats near the headline baseline with ~300px of empty column above it; the label rail reads as detached rather than annotating.
- Dev-server transfer was 7.0MB unminified across 11 resources; this is not a production number and `next build` was deliberately not run.
